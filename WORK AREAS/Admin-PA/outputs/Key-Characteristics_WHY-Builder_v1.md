# THE WHY BUILDER
**Topic: 3 Key Characteristics**
*Build the case for change before you make the case for your idea*

---

## FRUSTRATIONS

- They have "core values" on the wall. No one can name all of them. No one lives by them.
- They hired someone with "so much heart" — and that person quietly tanked the team culture.
- They've had the values conversation six times. Nothing changed after any of them.
- When someone isn't a fit, they don't have language for why. So the hard conversation gets avoided.
- Every few months they revisit the culture doc and it still feels generic, abstract, and borrowed.

---

## WANTS

- A team that executes with the same mindset without being told how to think.
- Clear, defensible language for who fits and who doesn't — before the wrong hire costs them a year.
- A culture that actually filters who joins and who stays.
- Winning looks like everyone knowing what's expected — and living it without being managed.
- A framework for the "this person isn't working" conversation that doesn't feel personal.

---

## MISTAKES

- They wrote values that apply to every organization on earth. "Be honest. Be passionate. Be reliable." That's a job requirement, not a characteristic.
- They confused character (who you are as a human) with capacity (what this mission requires of you).
- They have 7+ values. No one can name them all. So no one uses them.
- They never ranked them. When two values conflict — and they will — no one knows which wins.
- They put values on the wall instead of embedding them in every hire, every review, every hard conversation.

---

## RESEARCH / DATA

- People remember lists of 3. Not 7. Not 10. Not 5.
- Generic values — passionate, reliable, truthful — appear in virtually every organization's culture doc. They're undifferentiated.
- StoryBrand's 3 characteristics: Profit Minded (#1), Intelligent Communicator (#2), Driven (#3). Ranked. Mission-specific. Eliminative.

---

## DEEP INSIGHTS

- The problem isn't that your team lacks values. It's that you've never defined which ones matter MOST for THIS mission.
- Key Characteristics aren't who your team IS. They're who your team must BECOME to win.
- Core values describe character. Key Characteristics describe capacity — what the mission demands.
- You cannot rank all values equally. Something has to come first when two values collide. That collision WILL happen. Most pastors have no answer ready.
- The moment a pastor names their Key Characteristics in rank order, they suddenly have the language for every hard conversation they've been avoiding.

---

## FRESH TAKES

- Your core values could belong to any church in America. If that's true, they're not YOUR values.
- "Passionate" and "reliable" aren't characteristics — they're baseline job requirements. Minimum wage employees are expected to be reliable.
- The real question is never "are they a good person?" It's: "do they have what THIS mission requires?"
- Most pastors are building their team on vibes. Then they wonder why the culture keeps drifting.
- Key Characteristics aren't inspirational. They're eliminative. They tell you who CAN participate and who CAN'T.

---

## CASE STUDIES

- **StoryBrand:** Profit Minded → Intelligent Communicator → Driven. Three. Ranked. Mission-specific. They didn't pick "be kind" or "work hard." They picked what their mission actually requires.
- **The pastor who went from 7 values to 3 characteristics:** For the first time, had a framework for the hire conversation, the performance conversation, and the exit conversation — all from the same source document.

---

## STORIES

- The pastor who hired someone with "so much heart" and watched that person slowly drain every high-performer on the team. Nine months in. Too late by then.
- The moment a leader looked at their culture doc and realized it was lifted almost word-for-word from a leadership book — and could belong to any church in a 500-mile radius.
- The first time a leader used rank order in a real conflict: "We didn't let them go for being unkind. We let them go because week 8 and they still weren't Seek First to Understand."

---

## FACTS / DETAILS

- If your characteristics apply to every organization, they're not yours.
- A list of 3 gets remembered. A list of 8 gets ignored.
- Rank order is the thing everyone skips — and the thing that makes everything else work.
- You are inviting people to get transformed, not just to show up. The characteristics define what that transformation looks like.
- When you name your 3 and rank them, you've just answered: who can participate in our mission and who can't.

---

## IMAGES

- A job posting that could be copy-pasted to any church in America. That's what your current values look like as a filter — they filter no one.
- A filter vs. a welcome mat. Characteristics should screen people in and screen them out. A welcome mat does neither.
- The mission as a mountain. Ask: what does the climber specifically need to reach the top? Not what makes someone a good human. What does THIS climb require? That's your list.

---

## MY ONE WHY

Your team can't accomplish your mission if no one knows what the mission requires of them.

Key Characteristics answer that question — specifically, in ranked order, and without apology. Not "here's what makes a good person." Here's what this mission demands. Three things. In order. Either you have them, or you're working to develop them. If you won't — you can't be here.

That's not harsh. That's clarity. And clarity is what your team is starving for.
