# THE HOW BUILDER
**Topic: 3 Key Characteristics**
*Turn the idea into steps they can run without you in the room*

---

## THE STEPS

**Step 1 — Write the mission.**
One sentence. You can't define what the mission demands if you don't know what the mission is. Don't skip this. It's the filter everything else runs through.

**Step 2 — Brainstorm.**
What does your team need to accomplish this mission? Go wide. Get 10–15 characteristics on the page. Don't filter yet. Quantity first.

**Step 3 — Eliminate the generic.**
Cross off anything that belongs to any organization on earth. If a Fortune 500 company, a hospital, and another church would all put it on their list — it's gone.

**Step 4 — Circle the survivors.**
Whatever's left after the generic purge — these are your candidates. You're looking for 3–4 that are specific to your mission.

**Step 5 — Run the test on each.**
Three questions per characteristic: Is it necessary for THIS mission? Is it aspirational and achievable (not already mastered)? Is it specific enough to assess?

**Step 6 — Rank them 1-2-3.**
This is the step everyone skips. Ask: if two characteristics collide in a real situation, which one wins? That answer is your rank. Do it for all three pairs until the order is clear.

**Step 7 — Name each one.**
Not a single generic word. A specific phrase that means something in your context. "Seek First to Understand" is a name. "Caring" is not.

**Done when:** You have 3 characteristics. Named. Ranked. And you can explain in one sentence why each one is non-negotiable for YOUR mission specifically.

---

## THE DEMO

**Show it, don't describe it.**
Pull up the whiteboard. Write the mission at the top. Do the brainstorm live with the room. Then run the elimination round — cross off anything generic. Watch what's left. That's the moment they see it.

**Worked example (StoryBrand):**
Mission: Help companies clarify their message to grow revenue.
1. Profit Minded — because the mission is commercial impact, not just good communication
2. Intelligent Communicator — because the work IS messaging; you can't do this mission without it
3. Driven — because the pace is fast and half-effort doesn't ship

Notice: none of these belong to a hospital. None belong to a nonprofit. They're specific. They eliminate.

**One rep before they leave:**
Have them write their mission and brainstorm 5 characteristics right now. Then cross off the generic ones. See what survives. That's their starting point to finish this week.

---

## THE SEQUENCE

**Must happen first:** The mission sentence. Everything else is arbitrary without it. If they can't write the mission in one sentence, that's the real problem — solve that first.

**Shift point:** The elimination round. When they cross off "passionate" and "reliable" and see what's left — that's when it clicks. Slow down here. Let the silence land. Ask: "What's left that only belongs to you?"

**What can wait:** Perfect names. The rank order and specificity matter more than elegant language on day one. They can refine the names after they've run with the rank for a week.

**Pace:**
- Brainstorm: fast — just get it out, no editing
- Elimination: slow — this is where the real thinking happens
- Rank order: deliberate — this deserves a full conversation, not a quick vote

## THE WORKSHEET

**Prompts that pull the real answer out:**

1. My mission in one sentence: ___
2. Brainstorm — what does this mission demand of the people on our team? (Aim for 10+): ___
3. Cross off anything that belongs to any organization in your city.
4. Circle your top 3–4 survivors.
5. For each survivor, run the test:
   - Is this necessary for OUR mission? Y / N
   - Is this aspirational and achievable? Y / N
   - Is it specific enough to assess? Y / N
6. Rank them 1–2–3. If two collide in real life, which wins?
   - #1: ___
   - #2: ___
   - #3: ___
7. For each: Why is this required for OUR mission specifically? ___

**The artifact they walk away holding:** A named, ranked list of 3 Key Characteristics — each with a one-sentence explanation of why it's non-negotiable for their specific mission.

**Re-runnable:** Yes. Revisit annually or when the mission shifts. The brainstorm changes as the mission sharpens.

## THE TOOLS

**What they need in hand:**
- The worksheet (this one)
- A whiteboard or blank paper for the brainstorm phase
- Their mission statement in writing — not from memory

**What makes the hard step easier:**
The hardest step is eliminating the generic ones. Give them a simple test: "Would a hospital put this on their list?" If yes, it's too generic. Cross it off. That single question does most of the work.

**What turns it into a filter (not just a wall poster):**
Embed each characteristic into your hiring process. Every interview asks: "Tell me about a time you demonstrated [Characteristic #1]." Every performance conversation references the ranked list. Every hard exit conversation starts with: "Here's the characteristic we're not seeing."

**What removes friction from rank order:**
Ask one question: "If two of these collide in a real situation — someone is highly [#1] but weak in [#2] — what do you do?" Their answer tells you the rank. Don't let them say "they're all equally important." That's avoidance, not leadership.

---

## THE GUARDRAILS

**Where people go off the path:**

- They pick characteristics that are too generic (passion, reliability, honesty)
- They refuse to rank — "they're all equally important"
- They stop at 5 or 6 — "we couldn't narrow it down"
- They pick character traits instead of mission-required capacities

**Warning signs they're doing it wrong:**

- "This applies to any team" → generic value, not a key characteristic
- "I can't pick which is most important" → they haven't connected the characteristics to the mission yet
- "We landed on 6" → that's a list of virtues, not a filter
- The list sounds like a motivational poster → start over

**If this, then that:**

- If you can't explain why it's specific to your mission → it doesn't belong on the list
- If you have more than 3 → force rank and cut the bottom until only 3 remain
- If "they're all #1" → ask "in a real conflict between these two, which wins?" That resolves it every time
- If the name is too vague → make it a phrase, not a word

**Minimum that still counts as a win:**
You leave with 3 characteristics ranked 1-2-3. The names can be refined. The rank cannot be skipped. That's the floor.

---

## PATHWAYS

**Ideal path:**
Mission → Brainstorm (go wide) → Eliminate generic → Circle survivors → Test each → Rank → Name → Done.

**Costly detour:**
Starting with the characteristics you *want* to have instead of what the mission *demands*. This produces an inspiring list that functions as a welcome mat. Nobody gets filtered in or out. Nothing changes.

**What happens if they skip rank order:**
They have a nice list that collapses the first time two characteristics collide in a real decision. No rank = no resolution. The hard conversation still gets avoided.

**Shortcut for the experienced:**
Start from the disqualifier. Ask: "What would disqualify someone from this mission regardless of everything else?" Work backwards from the disqualifier to the characteristic. Faster path to the real #1.

**Rails for the new:**
Use the worksheet. Go through every step in order. Don't skip the brainstorm — you need to go wide before you can go narrow. The elimination round only works if you had something to eliminate.

---

## MY ONE PROCESS

Start with your mission.

Brainstorm everything it demands of your team. Cross off what any org could claim. Test what's left. Rank it. Name it. Three characteristics. In order.

You now have a filter — not a welcome mat. Every hire, every review, every hard conversation runs through those three things in that order.

That's the process. It takes 45 minutes to run. It takes years to actually use.
