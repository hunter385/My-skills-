# THE WHAT BUILDER
**Topic: 3 Key Characteristics**
*Name the idea so clearly they could teach it to someone else*

---

## THE POINT

Your team doesn't need better values. They need the 3 characteristics your mission actually requires.

- **Most people think X:** "We have core values" means we have a culture.
- **But actually Y:** Core values describe who you want to be. Key Characteristics define what your mission demands of the people who will accomplish it.
- **Under 10 words:** Three ranked characteristics. Everything else is just a welcome mat.
- **The claim:** Every mission demands specific capacity. Generic values don't create it.

---

## THE FRAMEWORK

**Three tests. In order.**

1. **Necessary** — Is this characteristic required to accomplish THIS mission? If it applies to every org on earth, it doesn't qualify.
2. **Aspirational and achievable** — Does it invite people to grow? You're not hiring finished products. You're inviting people into a transformation.
3. **Ranked in order** — When two characteristics conflict — and they will — rank order tells everyone which one wins. Without rank, you're guessing.

**Name it:** The Key Characteristics Test — or simply: Three. Ranked. Mission-specific.

**Drawable from memory:** Yes. Three boxes, stacked, numbered 1–2–3. That's it.

---

## THE REFRAME

- **Old belief replaced:** Culture is a feeling you cultivate. New belief: Culture is a filter you design.
- **What they see differently:** They stop asking "is this person a good human?" and start asking "does this person have what THIS mission requires?"
- **Obvious in hindsight:** The moment you rank your characteristics, you suddenly have language for every hard conversation you've been avoiding for months.
- **What shifts:** They stop managing around the misfit and start defining who can participate in the mission — before the misfit is on payroll.

---

## THE MODEL

**Shape:** A ranked vertical stack — 1, 2, 3. Not a circle. Not a Venn. A list with a clear top.

**Moving parts:**
- Mission → defines what the work demands
- 3 Characteristics (ranked) → translates that demand into human capacity
- Filter → answers who can participate and who can't

**Where it starts:** The mission. Not the person.
**Where it ends:** A ranked list of 3 characteristics you can use in every hire, every review, every hard conversation.

**Drawable in 30 seconds:** Yes. Write the mission at the top. Draw an arrow down. Write 1, 2, 3. Done.

---

## THE METAPHOR

- **The mountain:** Every mountain demands specific gear. You don't bring the same equipment to Everest that you'd bring to a day hike. What does YOUR mountain require? That's your list.
- **Filter vs. welcome mat:** Most core values function as a welcome mat — they invite everyone in. Key Characteristics are a filter — they tell you who can stay and who can't enter.
- **The job spec for the mission:** You're not writing a job description for a role. You're writing a spec for what the mission itself demands. The mission is the hiring manager.
- **What they'd repeat to a friend:** "He said most churches have values that could belong to any church in America. That's not culture — that's a welcome mat."

---

## THE EXPLANATION

**Why it works:**
When characteristics are specific to the mission, they're eliminative. "Seek First to Understand" is specific. "Be kind" is not. Specificity creates a filter. A filter creates clarity. Clarity makes every hard conversation easier because the standard was set before the conversation started.

**Rank order works because:**
Values always collide eventually. Rank order resolves the collision without drama. "We let them go because they weren't demonstrating #1 — not because they were a bad person." That sentence is only possible if you've ranked.

**What has to be true for this to hold:**
The characteristics must be genuinely tied to the mission — not borrowed, not generic, not aspirational in the abstract. If you can't explain WHY this characteristic is necessary for THIS mission, it doesn't belong on the list.

**Where it breaks:**
If you pick characteristics that are too vague (passionate, reliable, driven), they lose all filter power. Vague characteristics can't be assessed, can't be defended, and can't drive a hard conversation. Specificity is the whole mechanism.

---

## DISTINCTIONS

**Key Characteristics ARE:**
- Mission-specific
- Ranked in order
- Focused on capacity (what the mission demands)
- A filter — they tell you who can participate and who can't
- Aspirational — they invite people to grow into them

**Key Characteristics are NOT:**
- Core values
- Personality traits
- Generic virtues (honesty, passion, reliability)
- A welcome mat
- A list of nice things to be

**What everyone confuses this with:** Core values. Here's the line — Core values describe who you are as a human. Key Characteristics describe what THIS mission requires of you. One is about character. The other is about capacity.

**The precise line:** If your characteristics could belong to any organization, they're not Key Characteristics. They're core values at best. Key Characteristics are eliminative by design — they name who can be here and who can't.

---

## MY ONE IDEA

Three ranked characteristics, specific to your mission, is the difference between a culture that drifts and a team that executes.

Not seven values. Not generic virtues. Three. Ranked. Mission-specific. That's the whole idea.
