# Ikigai → Action

Revisit each cooldown.

The whole point: you're a framework-builder who got paid to coach. Every step below moves you off "sell my time" and onto "build once, sell many."

---

## The action steps

1. **Pick one framework to productize first.** Growth Plan or Middle Method. One bet, not a buffet. The platform starts as a single product.

2. **Build the "without me" version.** Turn that framework into something that delivers when you're not in the room — a course, a tool, or an incubator cohort. If it needs you present, it's not the product yet.

3. **Pre-sell it before you build it.** Sell one cohort or one course to a small list. Validation is a wire transfer, not a survey.

4. **Hand off the delivery that drains you.** List every paid thing that requires Hunter-in-the-room. Assign or template what you can. Your genius is invention — stop spending hours in the phase you lose interest in.

5. **Name the bridge with your dad out loud.** Decide what stays RSG and what becomes the platform, and on what timeline. The direction is mostly settled. The unspoken transition is what keeps it stuck.

6. **Clone NeoWorld University for yourself.** You already built the leveraged-membership model — for Dillon. Map how it works and run the same play for entrepreneurs.

---

## The questions to ask yourself

1. **Am I selling my time or my frameworks?** Which do I want my next decade built on?

2. **If it paid nothing, would I still coach churches?** The honest answer sets the timeline.

3. **What would I build if I weren't afraid of disappointing my dad?** Name the thing I'm editing out to keep the peace.

4. **Would I still want this if no one was impressed by it?** Run every goal through it.

5. **Does this buy me freedom or cost me freedom?** A platform that eats more of my calendar isn't the win — even if the revenue says it is.

6. **What's the one framework that, if it became a product, makes everything else optional?** Build that first.
