# The RSG Short-Form Playbook

The standing system for every short-form video. Read it before you script. Run it every week.

Default structure: **HEIT.** Hook. Explain. Illustrate. Teach.

This replaces HOOK → BUILD → PAYOFF → INVITATION. One spine now. No improvising on the fly.

---

## THE ONE RULE

People don't follow accounts. They follow people who make them feel understood.

Every video has one job: make the viewer think *"that's exactly what I'm going through"* inside the first two seconds.

Miss the first two seconds and nothing else matters. Not the framework. Not the close. Not the edit.

The hook is the whole game.

---

## PART 1 — PICK THE TOPIC

Don't script a word until the topic earns it. Guessing topics is how you burn out posting content nobody watches.

A topic earns a script when it passes four tests.

**1. FOS — does it land on all three layers?**

Every topic has to work for three people at once:

- **Fans** — already get the niche, watch everything.
- **Orbiters** — follow you, don't watch every video.
- **Strangers** — never seen your face.

Only works for fans? It won't grow the account. Only works for strangers? It won't deepen trust.

For pastors: the messy middle, team alignment, the difference between vision and strategy, why momentum dies mid-cycle. All three layers get something.

**2. Proof of demand — is anyone actually asking?**

Don't invent demand. Find it.

- Comments on competitor videos.
- The DMs and questions your own audience sends.
- Reddit threads, forums, Facebook groups for pastors.
- Google autocomplete.
- Outlier videos — posts that got 5 to 10x a creator's average.

Nobody asking about it? Nobody watches a video on it.

**3. Repeatable — can you get five videos out of it?**

One topic, five angles. Swap the format, not the message.

Reel. Long-form breakdown. Carousel. Live Q&A. Story series.

Can you only make one video about it? Then it's not a topic. It's a one-liner.

**4. Novel but familiar — close enough to relate, fresh enough to stop the scroll.**

Don't say what everyone else says. Package the familiar idea in a contrarian or reframed way.

Weak: "Consistency is key on social media."

Strong: "Consistency doesn't mean posting every day. It means being predictable."

That's the move. Take the thing they already half-believe. Flip it.

## PART 2 — HEIT (the script spine)

Every video. No exceptions.

### H — Hook

One line. Not two. Not a line plus a follow-up.

It does two things at once:

- Creates curiosity — they need to know what's next.
- Gives context — they instantly know what this is about.

A stranger scrolling at speed feels tension in the first five words. No setup. Pain or confusion hits immediately.

**Hook types:**

*Contrarian* — flips a belief.
- "Most pastors think their team is the problem. It's not."
- "You don't have a vision problem. You have an execution problem."

*Framework* — names a system or rule.
- "This is the 365."
- "There's a reason your team falls apart in week four. It's called the messy middle."

*Labeling* — if-then, calls out a behavior.
- "If you're dumping a new idea on your team mid-cycle, you're the reason momentum died."
- "If your job descriptions are older than your last building project, your team isn't confused. You are."

*Story lead-in* — opens on a real moment.
- "A pastor told me his staff meeting runs 90 minutes and decides nothing."

*Authority* — leads with credibility or borrowed credibility.
- "I've coached pastors across the 500 plateau for years. The ones who break it all do one thing first."

*Curiosity gap* — surprising fact or analogy pulls them in.
- "The team that runs one framework for 90 days beats the team with a new idea every week. Every time."

**Hook rules:**

- Sounds like something you'd say out loud to a friend.
- No exclamation marks.
- No vague teases — "you won't believe this."
- No filler — "here's the truth about," "let me break this down."

### E — Explain

Two lines max. What it is. Why it matters right now.

Land at least one concrete number or specific detail. "73% of staff meetings could've been a text" hits harder than "most meetings are a waste."

Skip anything they already know. Explaining the obvious wastes their two seconds.

### I — Illustrate

This is where most videos die.

Describe a real, observable moment. Something they've lived, seen, or sat through. Not a hypothetical. Not a generic scenario.

They should think: *"I have literally watched this happen."*

Strong: "Twelve people in the room. Nobody knows why they're there. The leader spends the first five minutes explaining what the meeting is even about."

Weak: "A lot of churches waste time in meetings."

The strong one is a specific moment. The weak one is a sentence anyone could write.

**Illustrate rules:**

- Escalate, don't toggle. Start accessible, end specific.
- Each example builds on the last.
- Show what it actually looks like for a real person.

### T — Teach

The payoff. It has to be doable.

Two points max. Four ideas? Pick the two sharpest. Cut the rest.

At least one is a *do-this-today* move — something they can run in the next ten minutes.

Strong: "Open your calendar. Cancel one recurring meeting that doesn't make a decision. That's your first hour back this week."

Weak: "Try to be more intentional with your team's time."

The strong one is specific, immediate, concrete. The weak one is advice from any leadership book.

Close on a punch. One line. Often just a noun: *The middle.* *The system.* *The team.*

## PART 3 — VOICE AND POV

Scripts that sound like everyone else get scrolled past. Voice and POV are why they follow you and not the thousand other people in the niche.

**Voice — how you say it.**

- Tone: calm authority. Conviction with restraint. Charisma without volume.
- Vocabulary: plain English. No Christian-ese. No corporate-speak. Contractions — wanna, gonna, kinda.
- Rhythm: Hormozi / Jason Fried sentence length. One-line paragraphs. White space.
- Energy: strategist who teaches. Not a hype guy. Not a vague motivator.

**POV — what you believe.** Pre-loaded. Don't rebuild it each time.

Two anchoring truths run through every video:

1. You don't need more ideas. You need a system that gets your team executing.
2. The problem isn't starting strong. It's not falling apart in the middle.

Core reframes you keep returning to:
- It's not a vision problem. It's an execution problem.
- A directional statement isn't a mission statement.
- The team is mirroring the leader's lack of clarity.
- Most leadership content is built for the start and the end. Almost none for the middle.

If a video doesn't reinforce one of the two truths, cut it or reframe it until it does.

---

## PART 4 — CADENCE AND PLATFORM

Consistency beats volume. Pick a pace you can hold for six months without burning out.

- Minimum: 3 videos a week.
- Growth: 5 to 7 a week. *(Current RSG cadence: five.)*
- Aggressive: 1 to 2 a day.

Master one platform, then repurpose.

- Instagram Reels — trust and community.
- YouTube Shorts — long-term search traffic.
- TikTok — reach and discovery.

One video per week carries the ManyChat CTA. Pick the one with the strongest natural pull — the clearest open loop or named resource. Close it: *"Comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send you [DESTINATION]."*

---

## PART 5 — WHAT TO MEASURE

Watch:

- Views per video — are people finding it?
- Follower growth rate — is the audience growing steadily?

Ignore:

- Likes. Vanity.
- Follower count in isolation. 1,000 engaged beats 100,000 passive.
- One viral video. Without a system it's a lottery ticket.

The feedback loop: your audience tells you what to make next. Every comment, DM, and question is a topic waiting to be scripted. The creators who grow fastest listen. They don't guess.

---

## PART 6 — THE 45-MINUTE WORKFLOW

One video, start to post.

- **Scan (10 min)** — comments, DMs, competitor content. Hunt for topics.
- **Select (5 min)** — pick one topic that passes the four tests.
- **Script (15 min)** — one HEIT script. Hook, explain, illustrate, teach.
- **Shoot (10 min)** — teleprompter or from memory.
- **Post (5 min)** — upload, caption, publish.

The system is simple. The execution is what separates the ones who grow from the ones who quit.

---

## THE TEST (before any script ships)

- Does the hook hit tension in the first five words?
- Did a real, observable moment carry the Illustrate section?
- Is there a do-this-today action in the Teach?
- Does it reinforce one of the two anchoring truths?
- Would you be embarrassed to say any line out loud? If yes, rewrite.

Don't post what you'd guess at. Post what you'd say to a friend who's stuck.
