# AN A-TEAM STAFF FINDS AND FIXES PROBLEMS — THE WHAT BUILDER

*Name the idea so clearly they could teach it to someone else.*

**Stage:** WHAT (Planet Builder: WHY → WHAT → HOW → NOW)
**Lesson:** An A-Team Staff Finds and Fixes Problems
**Audience:** RSG pastors (Breaking 1000)

---

## THE POINT
*If they remember one sentence, what is it?*

**Problems don't persist because they're hard. They persist because they're blind, unowned, or buried.**

- **Most pastors think:** my staff has a problem-solving problem.
- **Actually:** they have a problem-*seeing*, problem-*owning*, and problem-*spacing* problem.
- **Under ten words:** "See it. Own it. Make room. Then fix it."
- **The claim I'll stake my name on:** the same staff you have right now can find and fix what's been broken for three cycles — once you remove the three blockers.

---

## THE FRAMEWORK
*The 3 parts that make this repeatable.*

**Recommended name:** Name It. Own It. Make Room.
*(The three moves an A-Team makes against the three blockers. You lock the final name — alternates below.)*

| # | The Blocker (why it persists) | The Move (how an A-Team beats it) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Problem Blindness** — they don't see it, or they've accepted it as inevitable | **Name It** — say it out loud, make it real. You can't fix what you refuse to see. |
| 2 | **Lack of Ownership** — they see it, but it's "not my department" | **Own It** — someone volunteers. Nothing changes until ownership is chosen, not assigned. |
| 3 | **Tunneling** — too buried in urgent to touch it | **Make Room** — create slack. Margin is where problems become solvable. |

**Order matters:** Name → Own → Make Room. You can't own a problem you can't see. You can't make room for a problem no one owns.

**The redraw test:** they should be able to draw three blockers and three keys from memory on a napkin.

**Name alternates (you lock it):**
- *Name It. Own It. Make Room.* — action-forward (recommended)
- *Name It. Own It. Clear It.* — parallel "-It" ending, stickier rhythm
- *The Three Blockers* — diagnosis-forward; pair with the move triad underneath

## THE REFRAME
*What they see differently the moment they get it.*

- **Old belief:** "My staff can't solve problems — I need smarter people."
- **New belief:** "My staff is blocked, not incapable. Remove the three blockers and the same people start finding and fixing."
- **Obvious in hindsight:** the recurring problem was never hard. It was just blind, unowned, or buried — for three cycles straight.
- **The shift:** you stop hunting for better people and start removing what's blocking the people you have.

---

## THE MODEL
*The shape that holds it. Drawable in under thirty seconds.*

**Shape:** Three gates between a problem and a fix. A pipeline, left to right.

```
  PROBLEM  →  [ BLIND ]  →  [ UNOWNED ]  →  [ BURIED ]  →  FIXED
                  │             │              │
               Name It       Own It       Make Room
```

- **Start:** a real problem exists on the team.
- **End:** it's fixed — and stays fixed.
- **Moving parts:** three gates. Each one is locked until the matching move opens it.
- **The diagnosis:** a stuck problem is jammed at exactly one gate. Find the gate, apply the matching key.
- **An average staff** has keys to none — so every problem stalls at gate one. **An A-Team** carries all three.

## THE METAPHOR
*The physical image that carries the whole idea.*

**Three locked doors.**

Every persistent problem sits behind three locked doors. A name. An owner. Room to move.

Most staffs are standing at the first door with no key — that's why the problem never moves. An A-Team walks in carrying all three keys.

*Supporting image (gate 3):* the tunnel — headlights on the ten feet ahead, blind to the rest of the road. Slack is what turns the headlights up.

---

## THE EXPLANATION
*Why it works. Where it breaks.*

- **The mechanism:** every problem that won't die is stuck at one of three points. You can't fix what you can't *see*. You can't fix what no one will *own*. You can't fix what you have no *room* to touch. Remove the specific blocker and the problem moves — without anyone getting smarter.
- **What has to be true:** the staff has to be willing to name things out loud (no punishment for surfacing problems) and the leader has to protect slack instead of filling it.
- **Where it breaks:** applying the wrong key to the wrong door. Creating slack for a problem nobody can see yet does nothing. Naming a problem nobody will own just makes a list. Diagnose the gate *before* you reach for a key.
- **The edge case:** some problems aren't blocked — they're genuinely unsolved and need outside help. This framework gets a problem *to* the point it can be solved. It doesn't replace the solving.

---

## DISTINCTIONS
*What this is — and what it is NOT.*

- This is a problem-**unblocking** framework. It is NOT a problem-**solving** framework — it gets problems to the point where they *can* be solved.
- **Ownership** is volunteered responsibility. It is NOT assigning blame after the fact.
- **Slack** is reserved Quadrant-2 time. It is NOT downtime, laziness, or an empty calendar.
- **Naming** a problem is precise and ownable. It is NOT complaining — complaining is vague and ownerless.

---

## MY ONE IDEA

Every problem your staff can't fix is stuck behind one of three blockers: they can't see it, won't own it, or have no room to touch it.

An A-Team removes all three.

Name it. Own it. Make room.

That's how a team finds and fixes — not by getting smarter, but by getting unblocked.
