# AN A-TEAM STAFF FINDS AND FIXES PROBLEMS — THE WHY BUILDER

*Build the case for change before you make the case for your idea.*

**Stage:** WHY (Planet Builder: WHY → WHAT → HOW → NOW)
**Lesson:** An A-Team Staff Finds and Fixes Problems
**Audience:** RSG pastors (Breaking 1000)
**Framework spine (the WHAT, coming next):** Three reasons problems persist on a staff — Problem Blindness, Lack of Ownership, Tunneling. The fix for each: Name it. Own it. Create slack.

---

## FRUSTRATIONS
*What drains them every week. What they'd vent about if no one was listening.*

- "I'm the only one in the building who notices when something's broken."
- Every problem routes back to my desk to get solved.
- The same issues resurface every cycle — volunteer turnover, dropped follow-up, missed handoffs — and never actually get fixed.
- Staff meetings are status updates. Nobody brings the hard thing.
- We put out the same fire on Monday that's back by Thursday.
- "If I don't catch it, nobody does."

---

## WANTS
*What winning actually looks like. What they'd pay real money to fix.*

- A staff that surfaces problems before I do.
- Walk into a meeting and hear "here's what's broken and here's what I'm already doing about it" — without asking.
- Root causes fixed once. Not symptoms managed forever.
- To stop being the smartest problem-solver in the building.
- A team that owns outcomes, not just tasks.
- One cycle where the recurring problem doesn't recur.

---

## MISTAKES
*The confident move that quietly cost them. The "best practice" that made it worse.*

- Hiring doers instead of problem-finders — then wondering why nobody flags anything.
- Solving every problem themselves — which trains the staff to stop trying.
- Rewarding busy over fixed. The person fighting fires gets praised; the fire never gets prevented.
- Accepting "that's just ministry" as a real answer.
- Packing the calendar so full there's no room to think — then calling the team reactive.
- Treating problem-solving as a personality trait some people have, instead of a system you install.

## DEEP INSIGHTS
*What everyone believes that isn't true. The root cause no one names.*

- It's not that your staff can't solve problems. It's that three blockers stop them before they start.
- **Problem Blindness:** they don't see it — or they've accepted it as inevitable. "Every church deals with this."
- **Lack of Ownership:** they see it, but it's "not my department." Ownership is volunteered, never assigned.
- **Tunneling:** they're so buried in urgent that long-term thinking dies. No slack, no fixing.
- The root cause no one names: you've built a staff optimized to *survive* problems, not *solve* them.

---

## FRESH TAKES
*The conventional wisdom I disagree with. The counterintuitive move that feels wrong.*

- Conventional wisdom: hire smart people and they'll solve problems. Wrong — smart people tunnel too.
- The counterintuitive move: give your staff slack. Margin feels like waste. It's where every fix is born.
- A calm staff member is often outperforming a frantic one. Reactivity isn't productivity.
- You don't escape the tunnel by working harder inside it. You escape by leaving it.
- Naming a problem out loud is the most underrated leadership act on a team.

## MY ONE WHY

**Full version:**
Most pastors think their staff has a problem-solving problem. They don't. They have a staff trained to *survive* problems, not *find and fix* them — blind to some, unwilling to own others, too buried to touch the rest. An A-Team is built to do the opposite. That's the difference between a team that carries the mission and a team you carry alone.

**One-line punch:**
An average staff manages problems. An A-Team finds and fixes them. The gap is three blockers — and all three are fixable.

---

## BRIDGE TO THE WHAT *(next stage)*

The three blockers set up the framework cleanly. Each blocker has one fix:

- Problem Blindness → **Name it.** You can't fix what you refuse to see.
- Lack of Ownership → **Own it.** Nothing changes until someone volunteers.
- Tunneling → **Create slack.** Margin is where problems become solvable.

See it. Own it. Make room for it. That's how an A-Team finds and fixes.
