# FRAMEWORK 1 — NAME IT. OWN IT. MAKE ROOM.
### *The three moves that unblock any stuck problem*

**THE PROBLEM THIS SOLVES**
Most pastors think their staff has a problem-solving problem. They don't — the staff is blocked, not incapable. Every stuck problem is jammed at one of three gates.

**THE FRAMEWORK**

1. **Name It. Make the Invisible Undeniable.** Say the problem out loud in one specific sentence, with a number if you have one. *Replaces:* letting a problem become furniture you walk past.
2. **Own It. One Name, Not a Committee.** One person volunteers responsibility for the cycle. *Replaces:* diffused ownership where everyone cares and no one acts.
3. **Make Room. Margin Before Motion.** Block the Quadrant-2 time, then remove something to create it. *Replaces:* trying to fix inside a calendar with zero slack.

**THE PAIRED CONTRAST**
> *A named problem without an owner is just a complaint. An owned problem without room is just a wish.*

**THE DIAGNOSTIC QUESTION**
> "Pick a problem that's lived on your list for three cycles. Which gate is it stuck at — blind, unowned, or buried?"

**THE PROOF POINT**
You know it's working when staff bring you named problems with an owner and a date attached — before you ask.

**RSG FIT** — Chain: clarity + daily execution. Core truth #1: you don't need more ideas, you need a system that gets the team executing.

**THE PUNCH CLOSE**
> *Unblock the staff you have.*

**DRAW IT — Flow chart, three gates** *(30 sec)*
```
 PROBLEM → [ BLIND ] → [ UNOWNED ] → [ BURIED ] → FIXED
              │            │             │
           Name It       Own It      Make Room
```
A box for the problem, three gates left to right, a key under each. The redraw test: a pastor can sketch this from memory on a napkin.

# FRAMEWORK 2 — GO UPSTREAM
### *Stop rescuing the same problem. Find who's throwing it in the river.*

**THE PROBLEM THIS SOLVES**
Most staffs spend every week pulling the same problems out of the river — soaked, heroic, exhausted — and never walk upstream to the source.

**THE FRAMEWORK**

1. **Count the Rescues.** The problem you keep solving on repeat is the signal. The repeat is the data. *Replaces:* treating each instance as a brand-new fire.
2. **Walk to the Bank.** Step out of the current long enough to ask, "What keeps throwing this in?" *Replaces:* staying in the water, too busy rescuing to think.
3. **Fix the Source.** Solve the upstream cause once instead of the downstream symptom weekly. *Replaces:* infinite rescue.

**THE PAIRED CONTRAST**
> *Rescue without upstream leaves you with a soaked, exhausted team. Upstream without rescue leaves kids drowning while you theorize. The move is the bank — climbing out long enough to look.*

**THE DIAGNOSTIC QUESTION**
> "What problem did your team solve this week that you also solved last month — and the month before?"

**THE PROOF POINT**
You know it's working when a recurring problem stops recurring — it surfaces once, gets traced to the source, and drops off the list for good.

**RSG FIT** — Chain: priorities + clarity. Core truth #2: the problem isn't starting strong, it's falling apart in the middle. Reactivity is what breaks the middle.

**THE PUNCH CLOSE**
> *Go upstream.*

**DRAW IT — Path / river** *(30 sec)*
A winding river top to bottom. Mark "X X X" rescue points downstream, a stick figure climbing onto the bank, and the source upstream labeled "the cause." Three numbered beats: Count → Bank → Source.

# FRAMEWORK 3 — THE SLACK MATRIX
### *Where prevention actually lives on a staff calendar*

**THE PROBLEM THIS SOLVES**
A staff with no margin can only react. Prevention — important but not urgent — never happens, because the calendar is packed with urgent.

**THE FRAMEWORK**

1. **Name the Fires (Q1).** Urgent and important. Real, but endless if that's all you do. *Replaces:* pretending the fires aren't real.
2. **Cut the Noise (Q3 / Q4).** The false-urgent and the time-wasters stealing the room. *Replaces:* a calendar full of things that feel productive but prevent nothing.
3. **Protect the Room (Q2).** Important, not urgent — prevention, planning, fixing roots. The only quadrant that shrinks Q1 over time. *Replaces:* hoping margin appears instead of scheduling it.

**THE PAIRED CONTRAST**
> *Urgency without margin builds a team that's always busy and never ahead. Margin without urgency builds a team that plans but never ships.*

**THE DIAGNOSTIC QUESTION**
> "Look at your staff's last week. How many hours went to Q2 — important, not urgent? If it's zero, that's the tunnel."

**THE PROOF POINT**
You know it's working when there's a recurring, protected block on the calendar for prevention — and it survives a busy week.

**RSG FIT** — Chain: rhythm + priorities. Core truth #2.

**THE PUNCH CLOSE**
> *Q1 shrinks only when Q2 grows.*

**DRAW IT — Grid 2×2** *(30 sec)*
Axes: Urgent ↔ Not Urgent / Important ↔ Not Important. Four boxes. Star the top-right (Important + Not Urgent) and write "the room." That's the whole talk.

# FRAMEWORK 4 — THE "JUST MINISTRY" ICEBERG
### *The problems you've stopped seeing because you decided they're normal*

**THE PROBLEM THIS SOLVES**
Most pastors accept recurring problems as the unavoidable cost of ministry — so they stop seeing them. A problem you can't see, you never fix.

**THE FRAMEWORK**

1. **The Tip: The Symptom You've Normalized.** "Volunteers always churn." Visible, recurring, shrugged at. *Replaces:* mistaking a pattern for a fact of life.
2. **The Waterline: The Phrase That Hides It.** "That's just ministry" — the sentence that turns a problem into furniture. *Replaces:* curiosity with resignation.
3. **The Base: The Root You Won't Name.** The actual, fixable cause sitting under the resignation. *Replaces:* invisible inevitability with a named, ownable problem.

**THE PAIRED CONTRAST**
> *A symptom without a name stays furniture. A name without a root gets you a complaint, not a fix.*

**THE DIAGNOSTIC QUESTION**
> "What problem do you describe with the words 'that's just how it is'? Say it as one measurable sentence. That's your iceberg."

**THE PROOF POINT**
You know it's working when "that's just ministry" gets replaced by a one-sentence, measurable problem statement.

**RSG FIT** — Chain: clarity. Core truth #1.

**THE PUNCH CLOSE**
> *Nothing is "just ministry" until you've named it and tried.*

**DRAW IT — Iceberg** *(30 sec)*
Draw the waterline. Above: the symptom everyone sees. At the line: "that's just ministry." Below the surface, large: the root cause nobody's named.

# FRAMEWORK 5 — THE FIREFIGHTER TRAP
### *Why rewarding the firefighter guarantees more fires*

**THE PROBLEM THIS SOLVES**
Leaders praise the staff member who fights the most fires — so the team optimizes for fighting fires instead of preventing them. The incentive is backwards.

**THE FRAMEWORK**

1. **Spot the Hero Loop.** The person who solves the visible crisis gets the applause. *Replaces:* seeing only the rescue, never the prevention.
2. **Reward the Quiet Fix.** Praise the fire that never started — the prevented problem. *Replaces:* an incentive that only pays out for chaos.
3. **Make Prevention Visible.** Track root fixes, not just saves, so the calm operator gets seen. *Replaces:* a calm staffer looking "less productive" than a frantic one.

**THE PAIRED CONTRAST**
> *Reward the rescue and you train a team of firefighters. Reward the prevention and you build a team that needs fewer of them.*

**THE DIAGNOSTIC QUESTION**
> "Who got praised last month — the person who put out the big fire, or the person who quietly made sure it never started?"

**THE PROOF POINT**
You know it's working when a staff member gets recognized for a problem that never surfaced — and the team understands why.

**RSG FIT** — Chain: role clarity + daily execution. Core truth #2.

**THE PUNCH CLOSE**
> *Stop applauding the fire.*

**DRAW IT — Scale / balance** *(30 sec)*
A balance scale. Left pan: "Busy / Rescued." Right pan: "Fixed / Prevented." Draw it tipped toward Busy — then flip it. Reactivity isn't productivity.

# FRAMEWORK 6 — WHO OWNS IT BY WHEN
### *The one-line artifact that turns a named problem into a moved problem*

**THE PROBLEM THIS SOLVES**
Problems get discussed in meetings and die there — no single owner, no room, no date.

**THE FRAMEWORK**

1. **One Problem, One Sentence.** Named, specific, a number if possible. *Replaces:* vague group venting.
2. **One Owner, Not a Team.** A name on the line. *Replaces:* shared ownership that becomes no ownership.
3. **One Block, One Date.** Calendar room plus the next move with a deadline. *Replaces:* good intentions with no when.

**THE PAIRED CONTRAST**
> *A problem with an owner but no date drifts. A problem with a date but no owner orphans.*

**THE DIAGNOSTIC QUESTION**
> "Point at your last meeting's hardest problem. Can you name the owner and the date right now?"

**THE PROOF POINT**
You know it's working when every recurring problem leaves the meeting with a name and a date attached.

**RSG FIT** — Chain: daily execution + role clarity. Core truth #1.

**THE PUNCH CLOSE**
> *Who. What. When. Or it won't.*

**DRAW IT — Who / What / When table** *(30 sec)*
Three columns: WHO · WHAT · WHEN. One row per problem. This is the live artifact from the Stuck Problem Worksheet — the close of the whole segment.

## MORE SEEDS *(lighter — run /framework-architect to build any out)*

- **Survive vs. Solve** — *The staff you built reveals which one you trained for.* The deep reframe: a staff optimized to survive problems vs. one built to solve them. **Shape:** Two columns or scale.
- **The Lone Smoke Detector** — *When you're the only alarm, the building burns the night you're asleep.* Single point of failure in problem-detection; the fix is distributed detection. **Shape:** Hub / mind map (one center vs. many nodes lighting up).
- **Reactivity Isn't Productivity** — *The calm operator is usually outperforming the frantic one.* Misreading calm as low output. **Shape:** Scale / balance.
