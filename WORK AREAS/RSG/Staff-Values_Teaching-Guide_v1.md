# Staff Values Workout

### Build Your Key Characteristics — the people your mission actually demands

*Teaching guide for the coaching call. Built on the Planet Builder: WHY → WHAT → HOW → NOW. Teach each card in order. Flip, don't read. The goal isn't for them to hear your values — it's for them to walk off the call holding their own.*

---

## CARD 0 — THE CENTER

**Key Characteristics.**

The traits your mission demands of the people who will accomplish it.

Not who your team is. Who your team has to become.

> Say this out loud, then pause: *"By the end of this hour, you're not getting my list. You're building yours."*

---

# WHY

*Open here. Don't rescue them with the solution yet. Let the tension sit.*

## CARD 1 — THE FRUSTRATION

Most pastors have a wall with values painted on it.

Integrity. Excellence. Passion. Teamwork.

Then the wrong person sits across the desk and they can't say why it isn't working.

The values were on the wall. They just didn't filter anything.

**Here's the thing.** Your values can't help you make a hard call if any church in America could've written them.

## CARD 2 — THE WANT

You don't want a nicer wall.

You want a filter.

Something that tells you — before the hard conversation — who can climb this mountain and who can't.

You want every staff decision to get easier because the standard was set before the moment arrived.

That's what Key Characteristics do. That's why we're here.

## CARD 3 — THE MISTAKE

*Three mistakes. Name them. They'll see themselves in at least one.*

**Mistake one: they list character, not capacity.** "Be a good person" describes a human. It doesn't describe what the climb requires.

**Mistake two: they pick traits any org on earth would pick.** Reliable. Passionate. Driven. A hospital wants those. A Fortune 500 wants those. So they filter nothing.

**Mistake three: they never rank.** Two values collide in a real situation — and they freeze. They never decided which one wins.

> The problem isn't that your team lacks values. It's that you've never defined which ones matter most for *this* mission.

---

# WHAT

*The reframe and the definition. This is where the lightbulb goes on.*

## CARD 4 — THE BIG IDEA / METAPHOR

Your mission is a mountain.

Don't ask "what makes a good human?"

Ask: *what does THIS climb specifically require?*

Altitude tolerance. A read on the weather. The grit to keep moving when the summit disappears into fog.

That's not a personality test. That's a spec sheet for the climb.

**Your Key Characteristics are the spec for your mountain.**

## CARD 5 — THE MODEL

*The core distinction. Teach it slow. Make them repeat it back.*

Core Values describe **character** — who you are as a human.

Key Characteristics describe **capacity** — what the mission demands of you.

> It's not about whether they're a good person. It's about whether they have what *this* mission requires.

One is about the soul. The other is about the climb.

You can have great character and zero capacity for this specific mountain. Both are real. Only one is your filter.

## CARD 6 — WHAT GOOD ONES DO

A real Key Characteristic does four things. If it doesn't, cut it.

**Mission-specific** — it fits your climb, not every climb.

**Ranked** — you know which one wins when two collide.

**A filter** — it tells you who can participate and who can't.

**Aspirational** — you're inviting people to grow into it, not hiring finished products.

> You're not asking people to show up. You're inviting them to get transformed. The characteristics define what that transformation looks like.

## CARD 7 — SPECIFIC IS THE WHOLE GAME

"Seek First to Understand" is a Key Characteristic.

"Be kind" is not.

One you can assess, defend, and build a hard conversation on. The other is a vibe.

Specificity creates a filter. A filter creates clarity. Clarity makes every hard conversation easier — because the standard was set before the conversation started.

**Vague can't be assessed. Vague can't be defended. Vague can't drive a hard call.**

Pick vague, and you've painted another wall.

---

# HOW

*The live workout. This is where they build. Run them through it on the call — don't lecture it. Seven steps. Hand them the prompts.*

## CARD 8 — THE BUILD (7 STEPS)

**Step 1 — Write the mission. One sentence.** You can't define what the climb demands if you don't know the climb. Don't skip this. It's the filter everything runs through.

**Step 2 — Brainstorm wide.** What does your team need to accomplish this mission? Get 10–15 on the page. No filtering yet. Quantity first.

**Step 3 — Cross off the generic.** If a Fortune 500, a hospital, and another church would all list it — it's gone.

**Step 4 — Circle the survivors.** Whatever's left after the purge. You're hunting for 3–4 that are specific to your mountain.

**Step 5 — Run the three tests on each one** *(next card)*.

**Step 6 — Rank them 1-2-3.** The step everyone skips. If two collide in a real situation, which one wins? Decide it now, on the call — not in the moment it happens.

**Step 7 — Name each one.** Not a single generic word. A specific phrase that means something in your context. "Seek First to Understand" is a name. "Caring" is not.

> **Done when:** You have 3. Named. Ranked. And you can say in one sentence why each is non-negotiable for *your* mission specifically.

## CARD 9 — THE THREE TESTS

*Run every survivor through these. In order. If it fails one, it's out.*

**1. Necessary** — Is this required to accomplish THIS mission? If it applies to every org on earth, it doesn't qualify.

**2. Aspirational and achievable** — Does it invite people to grow? You're not hiring finished products. You're inviting them into a transformation.

**3. Specific** — Can you actually assess it? If you can't measure it, you can't defend it, and you can't build a hard conversation on it.

## CARD 10 — THE LIVE PROMPTS

*Read these out. Give them quiet time to write. This is the workout — they do the reps.*

1. My mission in one sentence: ___

2. What does this mission demand of the people on my team? (Aim for 10+): ___

3. Cross off anything that belongs to any organization in your city.

4. Circle your top 3–4 survivors.

5. For each survivor: Necessary for OUR mission? Y/N — Aspirational and achievable? Y/N — Specific enough to assess? Y/N

6. Rank them. If two collide in real life, which wins?
   - #1: ___
   - #2: ___
   - #3: ___

7. For each: Why is this required for OUR mission specifically? ___

---

# NOW

*Lock it in. End on the action, not the motivation.*

## CARD 11 — REMEMBER

Character is who you are. Capacity is what the mission demands. **Build for capacity.**

If your values could belong to any church in America, they're not your values.

You can't rank everything equally. Something has to win when two collide — and that collision *will* come.

## CARD 12 — THE ACTION

Walk away with **3 Key Characteristics. Named. Ranked.** And one sentence each on why it's non-negotiable for your mission.

Not five. Not a wall of words. Three you'll actually use.

## CARD 13 — NEXT

This week: take your ranked three to your team and run them against the last hard staff decision you made. Did the filter hold?

Then put them to work — in hiring, in reviews, in the next hard conversation.

Re-run this annually, or whenever the mission sharpens. The mountain changes. The spec changes with it.

> **Don't paint another wall. Build a filter.** Three characteristics. Named, ranked, non-negotiable. That's the whole workout.
