# 25 Staff Values — RSG
*Each value is Necessary (mission-specific), Growth-oriented (aspirational, not assumed), and Observable (coachable behavior, not a feeling).*

---

## We Treat the Guest Like the Point

**What this looks like:**

- Noticing the person standing alone before you check your phone
- Adjusting your plan mid-service when a guest's experience is at stake
- Remembering names and following up — because the second visit matters as much as the first

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Doing your job while the guest figures it out
- Treating hospitality as the welcome team's problem, not yours
- Assuming a warm room covers for a cold handshake

---

## We Know Our Numbers

**What this looks like:**

- Tracking attendance, follow-up rates, volunteer retention, and giving trends — not just effort
- Bringing data into conversations, not just stories
- Knowing when your area is growing and being honest when it's not

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Guessing at outcomes or reporting activity instead of results
- Avoiding numbers because they feel unspiritual
- Only tracking the metrics that make your area look good

---

## We Multiply Before We Need To

**What this looks like:**

- Actively developing the person who could do your job next
- Delegating real responsibility, not just tasks
- Building a bench before the gap opens

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Hoarding your role out of insecurity
- Waiting to develop someone until you're overwhelmed
- Calling yourself a leader without being able to name who you're leading

---

## We Protect the Weekend

**What this looks like:**

- Treating Sunday as the moment everything builds toward, not just another workday
- Solving problems Thursday so they don't blow up Saturday
- Being fully present — physically, mentally, and emotionally — when it counts

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Letting personal drama spill into service time
- Treating logistical problems on Sunday as surprises instead of failures in preparation
- Being technically present but checked out

---

## We Own Our Spiritual Health

**What this looks like:**

- Taking your own walk with God seriously — not just as prep for your role but as a person
- Knowing when you're running on empty and doing something about it
- Pursuing honest community, not just professional community

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Using the busyness of ministry as an excuse to neglect your own soul
- Looking spiritually healthy on stage but falling apart at home
- Expecting the church to disciple you while you disciple everyone else

---

## We Close the Loop

**What this looks like:**

- Following up on every commitment — even the small ones
- Letting people know when something is done, changed, or delayed
- Treating follow-through as a form of respect

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Saying you'll handle something and never circling back
- Assuming "no news is good news" when people are waiting on you
- Finishing the task but not telling anyone

---

## We Prepare Like It Matters

**What this looks like:**

- Showing up to meetings knowing the material, not catching up in real time
- Reviewing the run of show before Sunday, not during
- Doing the reps before the room fills — not winging it and calling it faith

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Overpromising what you'll have ready and under-delivering
- Treating preparation as optional because you're naturally gifted
- Spending more time performing competence than building it

---

## We Carry the Mission Off the Clock

**What this looks like:**

- Living like an ambassador when no one's checking
- Inviting people personally, not just posting about services
- Being the kind of person your neighbors, barista, and gym buddy would actually come to church with

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Treating your faith as a professional identity but not a personal one
- Going dark on mission the moment Friday hits
- Expecting the church to grow without personally bringing people into it

---

## We Recruit Constantly

**What this looks like:**

- Always having names of people you're moving toward volunteer roles
- Treating every great conversation as a potential open door
- Asking specifically, not generally — "Would you serve?" not "We need help"

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Waiting for people to sign up on their own
- Posting needs on social and calling it recruiting
- Only recruiting when you're desperate

---

## We Handle Criticism Without Collapsing

**What this looks like:**

- Separating feedback about the work from feedback about your worth
- Sitting with hard words long enough to find what's true in them
- Responding to critics with curiosity before defensiveness

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Crumbling or going cold when someone pushes back on your work
- Dismissing every critical voice as an attack
- Needing constant affirmation to stay motivated

---

## We Protect the Room

**What this looks like:**

- Refusing to let cynicism, gossip, or negativity spread through the team
- Addressing cultural drift in the moment, not after it's normalized
- Being the kind of person who raises the energy when you walk in

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Venting frustrations to peers instead of leaders
- Staying silent while someone else poisons the culture
- Letting private doubts become public toxin

---

## We Lead Up, Not Just Down

**What this looks like:**

- Giving your leader the information they need before they have to ask for it
- Anticipating what leadership needs to make a good call and surfacing it early
- Flagging problems with context and a proposed direction — not just the problem

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Waiting to be micromanaged instead of proactively communicating
- Sitting on concerns until they become crises
- Complaining to peers about a decision instead of raising it with the person who made it

---

## We Work at the Speed of the Season

**What this looks like:**

- Knowing the church calendar and adjusting your pace and priorities accordingly
- Leaning in during high-demand seasons without being asked
- Creating margin in slow seasons so you're ready when it spikes

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Treating every week like it has the same urgency and load
- Being blindsided by predictable high-pressure moments
- Burning out in crunch time because you didn't rest when you could

---

## We Build Volunteers, Not Just Rosters

**What this looks like:**

- Developing the people you lead, not just scheduling them
- Checking in personally — not just about the role but about the person
- Creating pathways for volunteers to grow into bigger responsibilities

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Treating volunteers as labor, not people
- Measuring success only by whether the role got filled
- Never investing time unless there's a gap you need covered

---

## We Speak Up in the Room

**What this looks like:**

- Sharing your honest read during the meeting, not in the parking lot after
- Asking the question everyone's thinking but no one's saying
- Trusting that the team needs your perspective, not just your compliance

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Nodding along in the meeting and venting to a friend on the way home
- Letting bad ideas move forward unchallenged to avoid awkwardness
- Confusing politeness with agreement

---

## We Are Excellent, Not Impressive

**What this looks like:**

- Doing the behind-the-scenes work well — not just the visible stuff
- Caring about quality even when no one's watching
- Finishing things at a level you'd be proud to put your name on

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Polishing what's visible while neglecting what's backstage
- Prioritizing stage presence over preparation
- Calling something done when it's only done enough

---

## We Stay Teachable

**What this looks like:**

- Asking for feedback before you're required to
- Changing your approach when someone shows you a better way
- Seeing correction as data, not a verdict

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Tolerating feedback but never actually changing
- Only learning from people above you on the org chart
- Treating tenure as a reason not to keep growing

---

## We Think Stewardship Before Spending

**What this looks like:**

- Treating the church's money like it belongs to the mission — because it does
- Asking "is this the best use of these resources?" before signing off
- Finding ways to do more with what we have before asking for more

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Spending freely because "it's ministry"
- Letting budget go unspent without asking if it could serve a better purpose
- Treating generosity toward your department as faithfulness to the church

---

## We Stay Humble About What We Don't Know

**What this looks like:**

- Saying "I don't know — let me find out" without embarrassment
- Asking questions before assuming
- Bringing in other voices when a decision is outside your lane

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Faking confidence to protect your credibility
- Making calls you're not qualified to make instead of asking for help
- Treating expertise in one area as expertise in all areas

---

## We Set the Tone Before the Crowd Arrives

**What this looks like:**

- Being fully set up, warmed up, and ready before the first guest walks in
- Creating an atmosphere of energy and expectation — not scrambling at the last minute
- Leading your team into game mode early, not at kickoff

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Arriving with just enough time to wing it
- Letting the room feel like a rehearsal when it's actually the moment
- Leaving the energy up to someone else

---

## We Ask Before We Assume

**What this looks like:**

- Clarifying expectations before starting, not midway through or after
- Checking in when something feels unclear instead of guessing and hoping
- Treating a short clarifying conversation as faster than rework

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Operating on assumptions and being surprised by misalignment
- Avoiding the question because it might make you look unprepared
- Reinterpreting unclear direction in the most convenient way

---

## We Finish Well, Not Just Fast

**What this looks like:**

- Handing off cleanly when a task moves to someone else
- Making sure the last 10% gets the same attention as the first 90%
- Not declaring victory until the outcome — not the effort — is complete

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Moving to the next thing before the current thing is actually done
- Leaving details for "whoever picks this up next" to sort out
- Calling something finished because you're tired of it

---

## We Take the Long View on People

**What this looks like:**

- Investing in someone's growth even when it won't pay off this quarter
- Giving people room to fail and try again instead of just giving the job to someone else
- Thinking about where someone is headed, not just where they are

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Writing someone off after one bad performance
- Developing people only when it's convenient
- Treating every team member as a resource to use rather than a person to grow

---

## We Guard Unity Without Silencing Honesty

**What this looks like:**

- Disagreeing with decisions through the right channels, not around them
- Being someone who can be trusted with hard conversations because you handle them well
- Choosing your team's trust over your own need to be right

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Protecting surface-level peace while letting real tension fester underneath
- Using "I just want unity" as a reason to never push back
- Lobbying others privately for support before raising a concern with leadership

---

## We Make People Feel Like They Belong Before They Believe

**What this looks like:**

- Lowering the barrier to connection — not just attendance
- Creating spaces where someone on the outside can feel on the inside
- Treating belonging as a value you build, not a feeling people find on their own

**What this doesn't look like:**

- Assuming a great sermon closes the gap for someone who feels alone in the room
- Only investing in people who are already committed
- Letting first-timers leave without a single meaningful point of contact
