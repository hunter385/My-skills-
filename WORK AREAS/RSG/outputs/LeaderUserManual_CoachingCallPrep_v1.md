# COACHING CALL PREP
## The Leader User Manual: Head, Hands, Heart
### Breaking 1000 | Group Coaching Call | 45 min

---

> **ASSUMPTIONS** — confirm before stepping on the call:
> - Program: Breaking 1000 (change if it's Breaking 500 or Double in 3)
> - Recent coverage: Unknown — confirm what Mark or Scott taught recently to avoid overlap

---

## BANTER NOTE (2 min)

After check-in, let the group talk. Wait for someone to describe the chaos in their week — the firefighting, the decision that should have been handled without them. It'll come.

When there's a natural lull:

> "I want to come back to that. Hold that thought. Actually — everything you just described is exactly where we're going today.
>
> But first: how many of you, if I asked your team right now what your top three priorities are, would get three different answers?"

[beat — let hands go up or let the silence answer]

> "Yeah. That's the room. Alright — let's get into it."

Tone shifts. No announcement. Just go.

---

## THE HOOK (1 min)

> "Every appliance in your building shipped with a manual.
>
> The HVAC. The dishwasher in the break room. The copier no one knows how to fix.
>
> The most complex system in your building — you — shipped with nothing.
>
> And your team has been writing your manual for you. In pencil. Through trial and error.
>
> Every error has your name on it."

[No explanation after that. Let it land. Move into the tension build.]

---

## THE TENSION BUILD (5 min)

Three beats. Don't rush. Let each one breathe before moving to the next.

**BEAT 1 — The symptom**

> "Right now, most pastors on this call have a version of the same problem.
>
> Every decision routes back through you. Nothing moves without you in the room. Your staff asks before they act — and when they don't, you wish they had.
>
> And you're frustrated. Because you hired good people. You paid real money for good people. And they still can't execute the way you would."

**BEAT 2 — The wrong diagnosis**

> "So most pastors do what seems obvious. They add accountability. They restructure the org chart. They bring in another tool — Planning Center, a new project system, a different meeting cadence.
>
> And it helps. For about three weeks. Then it drifts back to the same thing.
>
> You call it a competence problem. A motivation problem. A culture problem.
>
> It's not."

**BEAT 3 — The real cause**

> "Here's what's actually happening.
>
> Your team isn't slow. They're guessing.
>
> And here's the part nobody says out loud: they're guessing *rationally*.
>
> Your team is managing risk. The decision-making criteria only exists in your head. So every time they need to act, they have two options: guess and be wrong, or check with you and be safe.
>
> They're choosing safe. Every day.
>
> The root cause isn't your team. It's you. Not your effort — your documentation.
>
> You are the undocumented system your whole team is trying to reverse-engineer."

[Pause. Let them sit with that. Don't rescue the silence.]

---

## THE FRAMEWORK (15 min)

**Draw first. Name it second.**

> "Here's the framework. It's called the Leader User Manual. And the model is a person."

[Draw a stick figure — head, hands at sides, heart on the chest. Label them: HEAD / HANDS / HEART. Takes 30 seconds. Every time.]

> "Head. Hands. Heart. Top to bottom.
>
> Each section answers a different question for your team."

---

**HEAD — How I Think (5 min)**

> "The HEAD is how you think. What you value. How you define a win. What you'd do in a given situation, even when no one asks.
>
> When your team's in a meeting and a decision needs to get made — without you in the room — they run a mental simulation. They're asking: 'What would [pastor] do here?'
>
> If you haven't told them how you think, they're guessing.
>
> The HEAD answers: what do I value? what does a win look like here? what do I care about that most people wouldn't know to look for?
>
> When you document the HEAD, they make your call without you. Not their version of your call. Your call."

---

**HANDS — How I Operate (5 min)**

> "The HANDS is how you work. How you want ideas pitched to you. How you want work submitted. How you like to communicate — and how you don't. What done looks like for you before it hits your desk.
>
> Most pastors have never written this down. But they have a very specific way they want things done. And when their staff doesn't do it that way? Friction.
>
> The staff isn't wrong. You just never told them the rules.
>
> The HANDS answers: how do I want things done? how should you pitch me something? what does 'good' look like before you show it to me?
>
> When you document the HANDS, the execution matches yours — without you being in every room."

---

**HEART — How I React (5 min)**

> "The HEART is the relationship layer. What lights you up. What sets you off. How to handle it when things go sideways. What to do when they've messed up or when you have.
>
> This is the section most leaders skip because it feels personal.
>
> That's the point.
>
> Most alignment problems that look like performance problems are actually relationship problems. And most relationship problems between leaders and their staff exist because nobody ever named the unspoken rules.
>
> The HEART answers: what energizes you? what frustrates you? what should someone do when the relationship is under pressure?
>
> When you document the HEART, the relationship survives the work."

---

**The Recap (30 sec)**

> "Head. Hands. Heart.
>
> It's a person. It's you. On paper.
>
> The manual that ships with every system — except you."

**Diagnostic question to drop before moving into the worksheet:**

> "Here's the tell. If I asked your top staff person: 'What's the first thing you'd be uncertain about if you had to make a decision this afternoon without asking me?' — whatever they say is exactly what's missing from your HEAD."

---

## THE LIVE APPLICATION (15 min)

> "Here's what we're doing right now. You're going to start your Leader User Manual. Not plan to. Start it. Before we're done.
>
> Open a doc. Or write it on paper. Three columns: Head, Hands, Heart.
>
> I'm going to give you the prompts. Give them real answers. Not the answer that sounds right. The answer that's actually true."

---

**STEP 1 — HEAD (3 min to write)**

Answer these in the HEAD column:

- What do I value that most people on my team wouldn't automatically know?
- Who do I promote — and what does that person look like?
- How do I define a win on this team?

---

**STEP 2 — HANDS (3 min to write)**

Answer these in the HANDS column:

- How do I want work presented to me? (Draft? Final? Talk me through it first?)
- How do I want a new idea pitched? (Bullet points? In a meeting? Written first?)
- How do I communicate — and what does that say about how I want people communicating with me?

---

**STEP 3 — HEART (3 min to write)**

Answer these in the HEART column:

- What lights me up at work?
- What frustrates me — and how does it show up in me when I'm frustrated?
- What should someone do when they've made a mistake?

---

**SHARE BACK (4 min)**

> "Who's got something from the HEART section? That's usually the most surprising one.
>
> What did you write that you've never actually said out loud to your team?"

Take 2–3 responses. Quick acknowledgment. Don't lecture on top of them. Move.

---

## THE CLOSE (2 min)

> "Before next call — finish this.
>
> One page. One document. Three sections.
>
> Give it to your top staff person. Say: 'Read this. Tell me what surprised you. Tell me what you already knew.'
>
> That conversation alone closes 80% of the gap.
>
> You've been the bottleneck on your own team.
>
> Not because you're bad. Because you're undocumented.
>
> Stop making them guess."

[Nothing after it. Done.]

---

## COACHING TRIGGERS

---

**TRIGGER 1: "I don't want to be too rigid — I need my team to think for themselves."**

*What it looks like:* Said directly, or they go quiet and you can tell they're thinking it. Classic "I hired A-players, get out of the way" resistance.

*What Hunter says:*

> "That's a fair pushback. Here's what I think you're saying — if you over-document, you create a rulebook, not a team. And you're right that that's a risk.
>
> Here's what I've seen work: the Leader User Manual doesn't replace judgment. It replaces guessing. A-players still think for themselves — they just stop burning a quarter figuring out who you are before they can do it.
>
> Adopt it. Or adapt it to fit your context. Run it for 60 days and tell me what you saw."

---

**TRIGGER 2: "I've tried this. I did the values exercise. It didn't stick."**

*What it looks like:* They've done a values deck, a culture card, maybe a handbook. Nothing changed. They've got the cynicism.

*What Hunter says:*

> "A values list is the HEAD without the HANDS or the HEART. You told them what you believe. You didn't tell them how you work or what to do when the relationship is under pressure.
>
> That's why it didn't stick. The rules were still invisible. You documented the philosophy — not the person.
>
> This is different. The manual isn't your culture deck. It's your operating manual."

---

**TRIGGER 3: They're writing, but going surface-level — polished answers, not real ones.**

*What it looks like:* They're in the worksheet but you can feel the safe, generic, website-friendly answers. Especially in HEART.

*What Hunter says:*

> "Pause for a second. Whatever you just wrote in HEART — that's probably the version you'd put on your website.
>
> What's the version you'd tell your executive pastor at dinner?
>
> Write that one."

[If they need a harder nudge:]

> "What frustrates you that you've never said to your team? That's what goes in HEART.
>
> Not the polished version. The actual thing."
