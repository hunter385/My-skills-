# Leader User Manual — Visualizer Briefs

Five framework specs, formatted to drop straight into `/framework-visualizer`. Run them one at a time — paste a single brief, get one rendered HTML visual back.

**Color logic across the whole set (keep it consistent so they read as one family on screen):**
- **RED** = the broken state — undocumented, guessing, the wrong path, the miss.
- **BLUE** = the fixed state — documented, the manual, your actual call, the path that transfers.
- **Neutral (Base 900 / 600)** = structure, the leader, the team, the decision point.

Draw order in each brief = the order Hunter draws it live. One element per beat.

## VISUAL 1 — THE SABBATICAL TEST

**Framework name (header):** The Sabbatical Test
**One-line definition (subtitle):** Leave for 30 days, no contact — whatever breaks first names your most undocumented system.
**What it teaches:** The problem is personal and dateable. You don't have a vague "delegation" issue. You have one specific page you never wrote, and it has a name.

**Physical metaphor:** A structure under load. The team is holding up the roof. You step out the door. One support buckles first — circle it.

**Elements (4 max):**
1. **The running org** — three or four plain boxes holding up a roofline (decisions / comms / execution / money). Neutral, Base 900 line-drawing.
2. **The exit** — a door on the left, you walking through it. Label: *"DAY 0 — YOU LEAVE."* Neutral.
3. **The first crack** — one box buckles, a jagged crack drawn through it. **RED.** Circled.
4. **The verdict** — leader line from the cracked box: *"= your most undocumented system."* Red label.

**Color map:** Red for the crack and its label only. Everything else neutral. No blue on this one — it's a single-state diagnostic, not a before/after. (This is a deliberate exception to the red+blue default; use red + neutral only.)

**Punch close:** Whatever breaks first, you never wrote down.

**Teaching sequence (draw live):**
1. Draw the roof and the supports — "this is your org, running."
2. Draw yourself walking out the door — "now you're gone. 30 days. No phone."
3. Beat. Then draw the crack on one support.
4. Circle it. Write "= your most undocumented system."

**Teaching anchor:** "Whatever cracks first this month — that's the page you owe your team."

## VISUAL 2 — THE LEADER USER MANUAL (HEAD / HANDS / HEART)

**Framework name (header):** The Leader User Manual
**One-line definition (subtitle):** How you think, work, and react — on one page, so your team stops guessing.
**What it teaches:** You're a person on paper. Three sections, three questions your team is currently guessing the answers to.

**Physical metaphor:** A single stick figure, centered and large. Head at top, hands at the sides, heart on the chest. The whole framework is one drawable human.

**Elements (3):**
1. **HEAD — How I Think.** Leader line to the head. Sublabel (Caveat): *"They make your call — without you."* Label in **Base 900** (cognition = neutral/dark).
2. **HANDS — How I Work.** Leader lines to both hands. Sublabel: *"Execution matches yours."* Label in **BLUE** (process = calm, documented).
3. **HEART — How I React.** Leader line to the chest. Sublabel: *"The relationship survives the work."* Label in **RED** (emotion/relationship = red).

**Color map:** Figure drawn in Base 900 line. Head label neutral, Hands label blue, Heart label red. This isn't a broken/fixed split — here red/blue separate the three zones so the eye can hold them apart. (Deliberate, documented use; keep heart=red, hands=blue every time so it matches your other heart/process cues.)

**Layout:** Big centered figure occupying y=160–520. Three labels float outside the body with thin leader lines pointing in. Caveat sublabels sit just under each Lilita One label.

**Punch close:** It's you. On paper.

**Teaching sequence (draw live):**
1. Draw the stick figure — 30 seconds, every time.
2. Label HEAD, ask the question: "What would I do here?"
3. Label HANDS: "How I want it done before it hits my desk."
4. Label HEART: "What lights me up, what sets me off."
5. Step back: "It's a person. It's you. The manual that ships with every system — except you."

**Teaching anchor:** "Everything in this building shipped with a manual except the most complex system in it. So let's write yours."

## VISUAL 3 — THE TWO BAD OPTIONS

**Framework name (header):** The Two Bad Options
**One-line definition (subtitle):** With no manual, every teammate faces one fork — guess and risk being wrong, or ask and stay safe.
**What it teaches:** The bottleneck isn't a culture failure. It's the only rational move you've left them. They pick safe because you gave them no third path.

**Physical metaphor:** A fork in the road. One decision point at the top, two paths splitting down — and a third, ghosted path you never built.

**Elements (4):**
1. **The decision point** — a single node at top center. Label: *"A decision lands. You're not in the room."* Neutral.
2. **Path A — GUESS.** Branches left. Sublabel: *"Risk being wrong."* **RED.**
3. **Path B — ASK YOU.** Branches right. Sublabel: *"Safe — but you're the bottleneck."* **RED.** Circle it with a note: *"They pick this. Every time."*
4. **The missing path — THE MANUAL.** A third branch down the middle, drawn **BLUE and dashed** (it doesn't exist yet). Sublabel: *"The path you never gave them."*

**Color map:** Both real options in red — they're both bad. The missing documented path in dashed blue. The contrast is the whole lesson: two solid red wrong-turns, one blue path that isn't drawn in yet.

**Punch close:** They're not slow. They're choosing safe.

**Teaching sequence (draw live):**
1. Draw the decision point. "A call needs to get made. You're not there."
2. Draw the GUESS branch. "Option one — guess. Risk being wrong."
3. Draw the ASK branch. "Option two — check with you. Safe."
4. Circle ASK: "Which do they pick? Every time. Safe."
5. Draw the dashed blue path down the middle. "Here's the path you never built."

**Teaching anchor:** "Your bottleneck isn't a culture problem. It's the only rational move you've left them."

## VISUAL 4 — THEIR CALL vs YOUR CALL

**Framework name (header):** Their Call vs Your Call
**One-line definition (subtitle):** Undocumented, they make their version of your call. Documented, they make yours.
**What it teaches:** The gap between their best guess and your actual decision is every call you've ever had to walk back. The Head section closes that gap.

**Physical metaphor:** A target. Two arrows fired from the left — one lands off-center, one lands dead center. The distance between them is bracketed and named.

**Elements (4):**
1. **The target** — a bullseye on the right side. Neutral, Base 900 rings.
2. **Arrow 1 — THEIR CALL.** Lands off-center, outer ring. **RED.** Sublabel: *"Their best version of you."*
3. **Arrow 2 — YOUR CALL.** Lands in the bullseye. **BLUE.** Sublabel: *"Documented. Yours."*
4. **The gap** — a bracket spanning the distance between the two arrow tips. Label: *"Every decision you've walked back."* Red bracket.

**Color map:** Red = their call (the miss). Blue = your call (the documented hit). The red gap bracket names the cost.

**Layout:** Bullseye anchored right (around x=460–600). Two arrows angle in from the left. Bracket sits vertically between the two landing points with the cost label beside it.

**Punch close:** Document the Head. They make your call without you.

**Teaching sequence (draw live):**
1. Draw the target. "This is the right call in any situation."
2. Fire THEIR CALL — lands on the outer ring. "Undocumented, this is what you get. Close. Not it."
3. Fire YOUR CALL — dead center. "Documented, they hit this."
4. Bracket the gap. "See this distance? That's every decision you've had to redo."

**Teaching anchor:** "The distance between their best guess and your actual call is every decision you've ever walked back."

## VISUAL 5 — VALUES vs OPERATING MANUAL

**Framework name (header):** Values vs Operating Manual
**One-line definition (subtitle):** A values list tells them what you believe. The manual tells them how you work.
**What it teaches:** You didn't fail at the values exercise. You documented the philosophy and skipped the person. Belief doesn't transfer to a team — behavior does. (Pre-empts the "I already did this and it didn't stick" objection.)

**Physical metaphor:** Two cards side by side, each firing an arrow toward the same team. One arrow breaks and stops short. One reaches.

**Elements (4):**
1. **VALUES card** — thin card on the left, labeled *"VALUES."* Sublabel: *"What you believe."* **RED.**
2. **MANUAL card** — fuller card on the right, labeled *"OPERATING MANUAL — Head / Hands / Heart."* Sublabel: *"How you work."* **BLUE.**
3. **The team** — a small figure or box at the bottom center, receiving. Neutral.
4. **The two arrows** — from VALUES, a **RED broken arrow** that stops short of the team (*"doesn't transfer"*). From MANUAL, a **BLUE arrow** that reaches the team (*"transfers"*).

**Color map:** Red = values, the belief layer that stops short. Blue = the manual, the behavior layer that lands. The broken red arrow vs the connected blue arrow is the entire teaching.

**Punch close:** Belief doesn't transfer. Behavior does.

**Teaching sequence (draw live):**
1. Draw the VALUES card. "You've done this. Values deck, culture card, handbook."
2. Draw the arrow toward the team — break it halfway. "Here's why it didn't stick. Belief doesn't make the jump."
3. Draw the MANUAL card. "This is different. How you work. What to do under pressure."
4. Draw the arrow — it reaches the team. "Behavior transfers."

**Teaching anchor:** "You didn't fail at values. You documented the philosophy and skipped the person."
